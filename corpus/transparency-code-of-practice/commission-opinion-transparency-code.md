     1.    LEGAL BASIS

     (1)   On 10 June 2026, the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated content (the
           “Code”) was published. It was drawn up pursuant to Article 50(7) of Regulation
           (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and of the Council on a European approach
           for Artificial Intelligence (the Artificial Intelligence Act or “AI Act”). The purpose of
           the Code is to facilitate the effective implementation of the obligations regarding the
           marking and labelling of artificially generated or manipulated content as provided in
           Articles 50(2) and (4) AI Act. The Code also facilitates the effective implementation of
           the horizontal requirements for disclosure envisaged in Article 50(5) AI Act.

     (2)   Article 50(2) AI Act requires providers of AI systems (including general-purpose AI
           systems), that generate synthetic audio, image, video or text content, to ensure that the
           outputs of their AI system are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as
           artificially generated or manipulated, unless limited exceptions apply. The technical
           solutions for marking and detection employed by providers must also meet four
           requirements for effectiveness, reliability, robustness and interoperability.

     (3)   Article 50(4), subparagraph 1, AI Act requires deployers of an AI system that generates
           or manipulates image, audio or video content constituting a deep fake to disclose that
           the content has been artificially generated or manipulated. Subparagraph 2 of that
           provision requires deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates text which is
           published with the purpose of informing the public on matters of public interest to
           disclose that the text has been artificially generated or manipulated, unless limited
           exceptions apply.

     (4)   Article 50(5) requires the information referred to in paragraphs 1 to 4 of that provision
           to be provided to the natural persons concerned in a clear and distinguishable manner at
           the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure. The information must conform
           to the applicable accessibility requirements.

     (5)   Pursuant to Article 50(7) AI Act, the Commission may adopt an implementing act to
           approve codes of practice on the detection and marking of AI-generated and
           manipulated content in accordance with the procedure laid down in Article 56(6) AI
           Act. The Commission may also adopt common rules for the implementation of those
           obligations, if it deems that the code is not adequate. A contrario, it follows that the
           Commission has a corresponding obligation to assess the adequacy of the Code in
           accordance with the procedure referred to in Article 56(6) AI Act before proposing an
           implementing act to approve the Code, similar to the procedure foreseen for assessing
           the adequacy of Code of Practice on General-Purpose AI. In addition to a Commission

     2.     BACKGROUND

     (8)    Codes of practice play an important role in the implementation of the AI Act. While
            they remain voluntary, the Union legislature considers such codes as an effective means
            to facilitate compliance by providers and deployers of AI systems and AI models with
            their obligations laid down in the AI Act. The Code sets out commitments and measures
            to which providers of generative AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, and
            deployers of AI systems generating deepfakes and certain text may adhere to
            demonstrate their compliance with the obligations laid down in Articles 50(2) and 50(4)
            AI Act respectively and the horizontal disclosure requirements in Article 50(5) AI Act.

     (9)    The Code was prepared following an iterative drafting process.(1) On 5 November 2025,
            that process was commenced with the ‘Kick-off Event for the Code Plenary’ involving
            more than 180 participants representing eligible respondents to the call to participate in
            the drawing-up of the Code, launched by the AI Office in early September 2025. A
            variety of interested stakeholders were involved in the drafting process, including
            providers of generative AI systems, developers of marking and detection techniques,
            associations of deployers of generative AI systems, civil society organisations,
            academic experts, standardisation organisations and specialised organisation with
            expertise in transparency, and providers of very large online platforms and of very large
            online search engines designated in accordance with Article 33(4) of Regulation (EU)
            2022/2065. A list of those stakeholders and minutes of all meetings and workshops
            organised for the preparation of the Code are published on the Commission website.

     (10) The Code’s drafting process involved two working groups chaired by independent,
          academic chairs and vice-chairs with renowned expertise in the respective areas.
          Working Group 1 led Section 1 of the Code on Rules for the marking and detection of
          artificially generated and manipulated content applicable to providers of generative AI
          systems (Articles 50(2) and (5) AI Act). Working Group 2 led Section 2 of the Code on
          Rules for labelling deep fakes and artificially generated and manipulated published text
          applicable to deployers of AI systems (Articles 50(4) and (5) AI Act).

     (11) The Code sets out eight commitments, divided into two categories. Section 1 of the
          Code operationalises the obligations in Articles 50(2) and (5) AI Act with four
          commitments on marking and ensuring detectability of AI-generated or manipulated
          content applicable to providers of AI systems generating or manipulating synthetic
          audio, image, text or video content. Section 2 of the Code operationalises the
          obligations in Articles 50(4) and (5) AI Act with four commitments on labelling
          deepfakes and AI-generated or manipulated text published with the purpose of
          informing the public on matters of public interest (referred to as ‘published text’)
          applicable to deployers of AI systems.

     (12) Under Section 1, Commitment 1 sets out the marking of AI-generated or manipulated
          content with a set of measures. Commitment 2 sets out measures for the detection of
          markings of AI-generated or manipulated content. Commitment 3 describes the
          measures to meet the requirements for marking and detection solutions. Commitment 4
          addresses testing, verification and compliance.

     (13) Under Section 2, Commitment 1 sets out three measures to facilitate the disclosure of
          deep fakes and published text. Commitment 2 foresees three measures concerning
          internal processes for i) compliance, ii) awareness raising and literacy, and iii) review,
          feedback and cooperation with competent authorities. Commitment 3 sets out measures
          to operationalise the special regime for disclosure of deep fakes that are part of artistic,
          creative and similar works or programmes. Commitment 4 provides measures to ensure
          human review, editorial control and editorial responsibility for text published with the
          purpose to inform the public on matters of public interest applicable to deployers who
          wish to rely on the exception from the labelling obligation in Article 50(4) AI Act.

     3.   ASSESSMENT

          3.1.   Assessment criteria

     (14) The Commission must assess the adequacy of the Code pursuant to Article 50(7) AI
          Act. The purpose of the Code is to facilitate the effective implementation of the
          obligations regarding the detection and labelling of artificially generated or manipulated
          content laid down in Articles 50(2), (4) and (5) AI Act. The assessment is therefore
          whether the commitments set out in the Code ensure the “effective implementation” of
          those obligations.

          arrangements for making, as appropriate, the detection mechanisms accessible and
          facilitating cooperation with other actors along the value chain, disseminating content,
          or checking its authenticity and provenance to enable the public to effectively

          3.2.       Contribution to the effective implementation of Article 50(2) and (5) of the
                     AI Act

     (17) Section 1 of the Code aims to contribute to the effective implementation of the
          obligations imposed on providers of generative AI systems under Articles 50(2) and (5)
          AI Act.

     (18) Article 50(2) AI Act sets out a transparency obligation for providers of generative AI
          systems comprising three distinct, but inherently, interlinked elements. According to
          that provision, providers of generative AI systems must ensure:

                 •   that the outputs of the AI system are marked in a machine-readable format.

                 •   that the outputs are detectable as artificially generated or manipulated.

                 •   that the combination of the technical solutions for marking and detection meet
                     the requirements of effectiveness, interoperability, robustness and reliability,
                     insofar as this is technically feasible, taking into account the specificities and
                     limitations of various types of content, the costs of implementation and the
                     generally acknowledged state of the art, as may be reflected in relevant technical
                     standards.

     (19) Article 50(5) AI Act further specifies that the information referred to under
          Article 50(2) AI Act – notably the result of the detection – should be provided to the
          natural persons exposed to the content in a clear and distinguishable manner at the latest
          at the time of the first interaction or exposure.

     (20) The Commitments and measures set out in Section 1 of the Code cover each of the
          aforementioned elements of the obligation laid down in Articles 50(2) and (5) AI Act
          and facilitate their effective implementation by providers of generative AI systems as
          follows.

     (21) Commitment 1 facilitates the effective implementation of the marking element of the
          transparency obligation with commitments from Signatories to implement a marking
          solution with regard to the audio, image, video or text content, or any combination
          thereof, generated or manipulated by the AI system which is placed on the market or put
          into service in the Union. Measure 1.1 specifies the machine-readable marking
          techniques that providers of generative AI systems may rely on to comply with that
          obligation, including a multi-layered approach of at least two layers (metadata and
          watermarks) for increased robustness aligned with the state of the art and relevant
          standards, while allowing flexibility for a single layer approach if an equivalent level of
          compliance can be demonstrated by the provider of the generative AI system.
          Measure 1.2 outlines the commitments for markings not to be removed. This measure

          aims to advance the objective of effective marking, including through cooperation with
          other stakeholders in the value chain to preserve the marks.

     (22) Commitment 2 facilitates the effective implementation of the detectability element of
          the transparency obligation with commitments from Signatories to ensure that there are
          means to enable the detection of the machine-readable markings present in audio,
          image, video or text content, or a combination thereof, as generated or manipulated by
          their AI system. Measure 2.1 clarifies the requirements for the detection solution with
          regard to their availability, accessibility, personal data and security protection, and
          withdrawal. This is supported by recital 135 AI Act, which clarifies that the Code’s
          purpose is to support practical arrangements for making, as appropriate, the detection
          mechanisms accessible and to enable the public to effectively distinguish AI-generated
          content from other content.

     (23) Commitment 2 also facilitates the disclosure element of the transparency obligation
          with commitments from Signatories to ensure that the detection results are provided to
          natural persons that may be exposed to the content in a clear, distinguishable and
          accessible manner. Measure 2.3 further specifies this commitment with regards to the
          specific measures to implement clear and accessible disclosure of detection results to all
          natural persons in accordance with Article 50(5) AI Act.

     (24) Commitment 3 facilitates the effective implementation of the requirements for marking
          and detection solutions specified in Article 50(2) AI Act. Measure 3.1 details how to
          implement and assess in practice marking and detection solutions that are effective.
          Measure 3.2. details how to implement and assess in practice marking and detection
          solutions that are reliable. Measure 3.3 details how to implement and assess in practice
          marking and detection solutions that are robust. Measure 3.4 sets out a staged approach
          to the implementation of the watermarking and detection solutions that are
          interoperable. The latter measure includes clear commitments for the use of available
          open metadata standards, starting from the entry into application of the rules, and a
          minimum level of interoperability solutions for the watermarking detection mechanism
          to be implemented by 2 February 2027 to reduce the burden on providers of AI systems
          and enable a more seamless access for users. Such an approach constitutes a pragmatic
          step forward in a context where the state of the art does not yet support more mature and
          standardised solutions for watermarking and where the current ecosystem is fragmented
          and requires a certain level of protection to preserve the robustness of watermarking
          solutions. Furthermore, Signatories commit to cooperate towards advancing
          interoperability of watermarking and detection solutions and to implement improved
          and state of the art interoperability solutions and standards in a timely manner once they
          emerge. The Commission will monitor the implementation of this measure under the
          Code and reserves its right, in case of a lack of progress, to consider taking appropriate
          measures.

     (25) Commitment 4 facilitates the effective implementation of the transparency obligation
          under Articles 50(2) and (5) AI Act as a whole with commitments from Signatories to
          set up, keep up to date, and implement compliance, testing, verification, and monitoring
          processes to ensure the effective implementation of and to demonstrate compliance with
          the obligations set out in that provision and the commitments and the measures
          envisaged in Section 1 of the Code. Measure 4.1 specifies that Signatories will
          document, implement, and keep up to date, in line with the state of the art, a compliance
          process that describes, at a high-level, how they have implemented the different

          effectiveness of those solutions. All these measures are also motivated by recital 135 AI

          along the value chain, since these measures encourage cooperation with model
          providers, downstream deployers, end-users, civil society organisations, academia,
          independent researchers, providers of online platforms and search engines, market
          surveillance authorities, and other regulators.

     (27) Furthermore, Section 1 of the Code allows providers not covered by the transparency
          obligation laid down in Article 50(2) AI Act, such as providers of AI models and third-
          party providers of marking techniques and detection mechanisms that are provided
          independently from the AI system, to adhere to the Code as specified in the introduction
          to the Commitments. Those providers can also adhere on a voluntary basis and rely on
          the Code to demonstrate that their AI model or marking and detection solutions comply

          compliance for downstream providers of generative AI systems. The Code clarifies that
          references to Signatories throughout this Section of the Code shall be understood

          3.3.   Contribution to the effective implementation of Article 50(4) of the AI Act

     (29) Section 2 of the Code aims to contribute to the effective implementation of the
          transparency obligations applicable to deployers of generative AI systems laid down in
          Articles 50(4) and (5) of the AI Act.

     (30) Article 50(4) AI Act set out two transparency obligations for deployers of certain
          generative AI systems and their content:

     (31) First, deployers of AI systems that generate or manipulate image, audio or video content
          constituting a deep fake within the meaning of Article 3(60) AI Act must disclose that
          the content has been artificially generated or manipulated. Where the deep fake content
          forms part of an evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous work or
          programme, the transparency obligation is limited to disclosure of the existence of such
          generated or manipulated content in an appropriate manner that does not hamper the
          display or enjoyment of the work.

     (32) Second, deployers of AI systems that generate or manipulate text which is published
          with the purpose of informing the public on matters of public interest must disclose that
          the text has been artificially generated or manipulated. This obligation does not apply
          where the AI-generated or manipulated content has undergone a process of human
          review or editorial control, and where a natural or legal person holds editorial
          responsibility for the publication.

     (33) Article 50(5) AI Act further specifies that the information referred to under
          Article 50(4) AI Act (i.e. the applied measure that discloses that content has been
          artificially generated or manipulated) should be provided to the natural persons exposed
          to the content in a clear and distinguishable manner at the latest at the time of first
          exposure to the content.

     (34) The Commitments and measures set out in Section 2 of the Code cover each of the
          aforementioned elements of the obligation laid down in Articles 50(4) and (5) AI Act
          and facilitate their effective implementation by deployers of generative AI systems as
          follows.

     (35) Commitment 1 facilitates the consistent and effective disclosure of the artificial origin
          of deep fakes or published text on matters of public interest. It does so, in particular, by
          providing (i) three common EU icons (provided in Annex 1 of Section 2) that
          Signatories may use to ensure disclosure and (ii) common design specifications on
          which Signatories not using the common EU icon should rely, while allowing for
          certain degree of flexibility. These design specifications (Measure 1.1) are further
          complemented with placement specifications (Measure 1.2) that ensure clear and
          distinguishable placement of the icons or labels used by the Signatories, at the latest at

           the time of the exposure of the natural persons to the content, and that facilitate the
           effective implementation of Article 50(5) AI Act. Both sets of specifications rely on
           whether visual disclosure is possible. This ensures that the specifications are flexible
           and can be implemented across a range of use cases, sectors, and types of modalities,
           facilitating effective compliance by Signatories with Articles 50 (4) and (5) AI Act.

     (36) Commitment 2 facilitates the effective implementation of the disclosure obligations laid
          down in Article 50(4) AI Act by ensuring that Signatories put in place or maintain
          internal processes, awareness measures, and review mechanisms to that end. The
          implementation of the measures can rely on existing processes and documentation and
          take into account the size and available resources of Signatories. This ensures
          proportionality and simplification, without necessarily affecting effectiveness.
          Specifically, Measure 2.1 ensures that Signatories put in place or maintain appropriate
          internal compliance processes and documentation that specify how they implement the
          disclosure obligations. For Signatories that regularly use AI systems to create deep
          fakes or published text on matters of public interest, this is complemented with a
          commitment for a process to ensure and prevent potentially non-labelled or mislabelled
          content. Measures 2.2 (Awareness and literacy) and 2.3 (Review, feedback and
          cooperation with authorities) further ensure the effective implementation of
          Article 50(4) AI Act with commitments by Signatories to raise awareness and literacy
          among relevant personnel, to review observed or reported mislabelled content, and to
          cooperate with competent authorities.

     (37) Commitment 3 effectively enables deployers of generative AI systems to disclose deep
          fakes that form part of evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous work
          or programmes in a way that does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work,
          including its normal exploitation and use, while maintaining the utility and quality of
          the work. In particular, it does so by allowing contextual disclosure where the
          information can be provided through the broader informative context, rather than as part
          of the content itself, while still mandating compliance with the design specifications of
          Measure 1.1 and the minimum requirements stemming from Article 50(5) AI Act.

     (38) Commitment 4 facilitates the effective implementation of the exception to the
          transparency obligation under Article 50(4), subparagraph 2, AI Act. By making a
          distinction between media service providers as defined in Article 2(2) of
          Regulation 2024/1083 and other actors with respect to the measures to be taken, a
          pertinent distinction is made that does not impact the effectiveness of the exception. On
          the contrary, this distinction and the related expected measures ensure that the exception
          can only be invoked by Signatories that have taken appropriate practical measures
          supporting effective human review or editorial control, while also identifying a natural
          or legal person assuming editorial responsibility.

     (39) Section 2 further contributes to the effective implementation of Article 50(4) AI Act by
          optional measures under the Code that are not legally required, but voluntary and
          nevertheless recommended. These measures are conducive to achieving the goal
          pursued by Article 50(4) AI Act to enable natural persons to effectively distinguish AI-
          generated or manipulated deep fakes and published text on matters of public interest.
          For example, Section 2 enables Signatories and the associations of which they are a part
          to jointly establish common good practices for their sector or industry, which can
          provide further details for the implementation of the Commitments and Measures
          specified in Section 2. Sub-measure 1.2.1 encourages Signatories to collaborate, on a

          best effort basis, with actors whose services or products they use to further distribute or
          disseminate the deep fake or published text to preserve the icon or equivalent label with
          the objective of ensuring that the required disclosure accompanies the deep fake or
          published text throughout its further distribution. This directly serves the objective of
          the Code of Practice, mentioned by Recital 135 AI Act to facilitate cooperation with
          other actors along the value chain disseminating content and to enable the public to
          effectively distinguish AI-generated content from other content. Moreover, Measure 1.3

     (40) In light of the above, the Commission considers that Section 2 of the Code adequately
          facilitates the effective implementation of the transparency obligations laid down
          Articles 50(4) and (5) AI Act and that deployers of AI systems may rely on the
          Commitments and Measures set out therein to demonstrate compliance with those
          obligations.

          3.4.   Additional consideration for the facilitation of an effective implementation:
                 clear objectives, guiding recitals, commitments and measures

     (41) Each section of the Code outlines clear objectives and includes guiding principles that
          provide clarity to providers, deployers, and competent market surveillance authorities in
          the implementation of the commitments and the measures envisaged in the Code.

     (42) In addition, the Commitments entail purpose-clauses phrased as “in order to” or “for the
          purpose of” that set out the Commitments’ specific objectives (see, for example,
          Commitment 1 of each Section).

     (43) Moreover, measures distinguish between varying levels of legal commitment – using
          terms such as “will,” “encourages,” and “may” – to ensure clarity and eliminate
          ambiguity for future Signatories. These terms have been explicitly defined.

     (44) Section 1 enhances transparency by incorporating a glossary that defines key technical
          terms used in the Code.

     (45) Section 2 further supports practical understanding through an annex detailing the
          publicly available EU icon.

     (46) The Code’s Commitments and Measures outlined above are clearly directed to achieve
          their specific objectives described in their purpose-clauses and, in doing so, facilitating
          an effective implementation of the obligations under Articles 50(2) and Article 50(4) AI
          Act and the horizontal disclosure requirements in Article 50(5) AI Act.

          3.5.   Practical solutions accounting for the needs of interested parties

     (47) To facilitate the effective implementation of the transparency obligations, the
          development of the Code must account for the needs and interests of all stakeholders.
          This ensures that practical and workable solutions are identified, while enabling
          providers and deployers to sign up to the Code with ease.

     3.6. Code of Practice and Guidelines on Article 50 AI Act

     (49) Furthermore, the Code does not alter the Commission’s responsibility to provide
          guidance on the application of Article 50 AI Act. To provide clarity on the scope and
          legal concepts of the transparency obligations under Article 50 AI Act, the Commission
          will issue guidelines, as required under Article 96(1)(d) AI Act. Those guidelines will
          be relevant to understand concepts outlined in the Code and, in conjunction with the
          Code, will contribute to the effective implementation of Articles 50(2),(4) and (5) AI
          Act.

     4.   REVIEW AND ADAPTATION OF THE CODE

     (50) In accordance with Article 56(6) AI Act, the AI Office and the Board will regularly
          monitor and evaluate the achievement of the objectives of the Code and its contribution
          to the effective implementation of Articles 50(2),(4) and (5) AI Act, particularly in light
          of the rapidly evolving landscape of technologies, as well as risks of misinformation and
          manipulation at scale, fraud, impersonation and consumer deception, which both
          transparency obligations aim to address.

     (51) The AI Office will consider facilitating formal updates to the Code at least every two
          years, for instance based on the emergence of standards or relevant technological
          developments. To monitor the achievement of the objectives of the Code, the AI Office
          will remain in exchange with Signatories to understand where implementation support
          is necessary, and it may cooperate with national competent authorities, downstream
          providers, rightsholders, and other actors. It will also support the establishment and
          functioning of the task-forces envisaged under both sections of the Code, which will
          play a key role to advance the effective implementation of the Code and keep it
          practical, aligned with the state of the art, and future-proof.

     5.   CONCLUSION

     (52) In light of the foregoing, the Commission concludes that the Code of Practice on
          Transparency of AI-generated content adequately covers the obligations provided for in
          Articles 50(2), (4) and (5) AI Act and facilitates their effective implementation, thereby
          meeting the Code’s aims in accordance with Article 50(7) AI Act. Providers and
